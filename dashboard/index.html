      How each idea and its priority are decided

      Create web version: app-only article with 100+ app views in 4 weeks. It has no web page, so Google never sends it readers. If a web article with a similar title already exists, the row says so: add the content there instead of a new page.

      Fix the card: 2,000+ card impressions in 4 weeks and a card CTR under half the typical CTR of app articles. Rewrite the card title or change the thumbnail.

      Do SEO: web article with 100+ app views in 4 weeks but no meta title in the CMS, or under 10 Google clicks in 28 days.

      Check the drop: 30+ app views the week before and at least 40% fewer last week. If it was edited around then, the row says so: edits seem to drop articles from app recommendations.

      Priority is relative within each idea: the top 10% by payoff (app views; extra clicks for cards; views lost for drops) are High, the next 25% Medium. ⚠ marks articles with 300+ app views in 4 weeks: editing them may reduce their app reach.

      Articles to work on

      Article pages (/article/) with 300+ impressions in the last 28 days. Each dot is one article: further right means a worse position, higher means more impressions, bigger means more extra clicks if fixed. Click a dot to see that article below.

      Priority Article Fix type What to do Impr. 28d Pos. Extra clicks/28d

      How priority is worked out

      Losing clicks: clicks fell 25% or more week over week, from at least 40. Payoff is the lost weekly clicks × 4.

      Fix title & meta: position 10 or better with 500+ impressions, but CTR is under half of what our own articles typically get at that position. Payoff is the CTR gap × impressions.

      Move up: average position 3.5–15 with 1,000+ impressions. Payoff assumes it climbs 3 places (at best to #3). The fix names the next searches it half-ranks for.

      Rework for intent: position worse than 15 with 3,000+ impressions. Payoff assumes it reaches position 10.

      Typical CTR by position is the median CTR of our own article pages in that position band, so it reflects AI Overviews and other features on these searches. High = the top 10% of payoffs, Medium = the next 25%. Articles edited in the last 14 days drop one level so the change has time to show.

      The shaded band on the chart is positions 4–10: page one, but not yet top 3. Hindi URLs that Search Console cut off link to Search Console instead of the page.

      Redirect ideas: duplicate articles

      Articles with the same SEO title in the same language, compared word by word. Keep the winner as it is; 301-redirect the other copies to it. Winner = most Google clicks in 28 days, then impressions, then the fuller and older page.

      What to do Keep (winner) Redirect this page Same content

      How each idea is decided

      301 to the winner: 60% or more of the words are the same. It is a copy: redirect it, update links that point to it, and drop it from the sitemap.

      Merge, then 301: 30–60% the same. Move anything useful that the winner lacks into the winner first, then redirect.

      Fix the title: under 30% the same. The pages are different and only the title was copied: give this page its own title instead of redirecting it.

      Same content = shared distinct words ÷ all distinct words of the two bodies. Live from the CMS; Google numbers from the last 28 days.

      Pages competing with each other

      Pairs of our pages that each take 15%+ of the impressions on the same searches. Suggested fix per pair; open a row for the searches they share.

      Suggested fix Keep (stronger page) Other page Shared impr. 28d

      How the fix is chosen

      Merge + 301: same kind of page, same language, no different focus in the titles, the stronger page gets 3× the clicks or more, and at least 40% of the other page's impressions come from the shared searches. Move the useful parts of the other page into the stronger one, then 301-redirect it.

      Keep both, differentiate: the titles show a different focus (girl vs boy, a different language or community, a different week, month or trimester), or both pages are strong. Make each title and intro say its focus and link the two.

      Keep both, link: an article and a Q&A page, or the same topic in two languages. They serve different readers: link them to each other.

      Merged: the other page already 301-redirects to the stronger page (from the redirect check).

      How to read this

      App views/day: average app views a day (Android and iOS, opened any way) in the 7 days before the edit against the days after it (at least 3, up to 7). Card impr./day: the same for impressions of the recommended-article card. App data is refreshed on Mondays, so an edit is measured about a week later.

      Flags: Dropped from app after edit: card impressions in the 3 days after the edit fell to 20% or less of the week before (from 50+ a day): the app seems to have stopped recommending it. Bulk edit: saved in the same second as 20+ other articles, a mass update rather than a hand edit.

      Changelog

      Every edit to SEO articles and community Q&A pages, one row per page per day, newest first: what changed, before and after, and what happened to clicks. Saved daily from the CMS change feed, which keeps only 7 days, so history starts on the first day it was saved.

      Date Page What changed Body words Clicks/day before → after

      How to read this

      Body: the text changed (word count or H2/H3 sections). Formatting: the HTML changed but the words and sections did not (links, bold, spacing).

      Before is the page as it was before its first edit that day; after is the page after its last edit that day. An empty "before" means the field was blank.

      Clicks/day: average daily Google clicks in the 7 days before the edit against the days after it (at least 3, up to 7). It needs 3 days of Search Console data after the edit (Search Console runs 1–2 days behind), so an edit is measured about 4–5 days later. Pages with very little Google traffic and Q&A pages have no daily data; the cell says why.

      Flags: meta title changed, description not (the snippet may no longer match the title); much shorter (body lost more than 20% of its words).
